**Charter version:** 1.0.0  
**Decision date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** CONTROLLING BUSINESS AUTHORITY  
**Applies to:** HustleXP company strategy, Marketplace, Provider OS, Bring Your Own Provider, Task Opportunities, Recurring Work, Verified Trade Partner operations, `/OPS`, product copy, and implementation planning

This charter consolidates the founder-approved HustleXP business direction and the Universal V1 operating model. It resolves conflicts among older roadmaps, product specifications, underwriting material, audit records, READMEs, and implementation documents.

It is a business and product-policy authority. It does not claim that a capability is implemented, deployed, legally approved, processor-approved, or production-safe. Exact implementation and production facts must come from the repository and environment that own those facts.

## 2. Company doctrine

### 2.1 Internal company definition — LOCKED

> **HustleXP is a managed local-work transaction network. It converts legitimate customer demand into structured, financially committed, provider-fulfilled, evidence-backed, reconciled work.**

For general services and licensed trades together:

> **HustleXP captures customer demand and routes it to either qualified general-service providers or credential-verified local trade businesses through one governed operating system.**

### 2.2 Operating doctrine — LOCKED

> **Wide intake. Credentialed routing. Narrow financial commitment.**

> **Capture demand broadly. Commit narrowly. Complete safely. Reconcile every transaction. Convert successful work into repeat relationships. Expand only from evidence.**

Broad intake never means universal availability, universal eligibility, or automatic payment authority. HustleXP only promises, financially secures, assigns, and captures work that passes explicit geography, category, provider, risk, serviceability, legal, processor, and unit-economic gates.

### 2.3 Legitimate conversion outcomes — LOCKED

A qualified lead can produce any of six valid outcomes:

1. Fulfill now through an approved HustleXP lane.
2. Obtain a provider estimate.
3. Source manually.
4. Refer to an external provider under clear disclosures.
5. Waitlist while retaining the demand signal.
6. Decline clearly.

A lead does not need to become a HustleXP-paid transaction to create customer value or useful demand intelligence.

### 2.4 North-star hierarchy — LOCKED

1. Safely completed and reconciled paid work.
2. Repeat and recurring completed work.
3. Sustainable provider earnings.
4. Positive contribution margin after variable operations and acquisition.
5. Declining human intervention per transaction.
6. Expansion only after an operating cell passes its gates.

Lead volume, registrations, app installs, gross task postings, and provider signups are supporting indicators, not company north stars.

## 3. What HustleXP promises

### 3.1 Customer promise before confirmation — LOCKED

> **Tell us what needs doing. HustleXP will determine whether it can be serviced, structure the scope, and provide a clear price, estimate, referral, waitlist, or decline path without pretending a provider is already secured.**

### 3.2 Customer promise after confirmation — LOCKED

> **Once a job is confirmed, HustleXP coordinates an eligible provider, governs the agreed scope and approved financial lifecycle, records completion evidence, and supports the transaction through completion or a defined recovery outcome.**

The concise customer-facing expression is:

> **Get real work done.**

Across both marketplace sides:

> **Get real work done. Earn doing it.**

### 3.3 Licensed-trade promise — LOCKED

> **For regulated or licensed work, HustleXP helps customers reach a locally reputable, independently credential-checked business, organize the scope and estimate, and maintain a clear transaction record. The licensed business is responsible for performing the work, required permits, code compliance, workmanship, and its stated warranties.**

Until full trade payments are approved, the trade business contracts with and collects payment from the customer directly.

### 3.4 Provider promise — LOCKED

> **HustleXP gives providers access to real, scoped work with clear timing and disclosed economics; preserves voluntary acceptance; builds portable first-party performance history; supports approved payment infrastructure; and helps turn completed work into repeat customer relationships.**

For verified trade businesses:

> **HustleXP provides qualified local demand, structured intake, estimate coordination, scope and change-order records, payment infrastructure when approved, repeat-customer tools, and Provider OS without forcing businesses into an open-bid race to the bottom.**

HustleXP does not promise guaranteed work, earnings, category access, permanent eligibility, exclusivity, employee benefits, or uninterrupted processor access.

## 5. Product hierarchy and lane contracts

### 5.1 Product hierarchy — LOCKED

1. **HustleXP Transaction Network** — the company and governed operating system.
2. **General Services Marketplace** — acquires HustleXP-originated customer relationships.
3. **Verified Trade Partner Network** — routes regulated demand to verified trade businesses.
4. **Provider OS** — helps providers retain, operate, and expand customer relationships.
5. **Bring Your Own Provider (BYOP)** — brings pre-matched relationships into the governed system.
6. **Task Opportunities** — resolves supply and acquires providers around real Task Drafts.
7. **Recurring Work** — increases retention and transaction density.

These are not separate companies or competing lifecycles.

### 5.2 Commercial and operating differences — LOCKED

| Lane | Demand origin | Provider discovery | Scope and price | Relationship attribution | Commercial intent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marketplace | HustleXP acquires customer | Usually required | Platform-priced or provider-estimate | `MARKETPLACE` | Highest fee because HustleXP supplies discovery and orchestration |
| Provider OS | Provider brings customer | Not required | Provider estimate; HX governs versions and gates | `PROVIDER_OS` | Lower transaction fee; no V1 subscription |
| BYOP | Customer arrives with provider | Not required | Jointly structured through HX | `BRING_YOUR_OWN_PROVIDER` | Infrastructure fee below Marketplace |
| Task Opportunity | Derived from a real open Task Draft | Supply acquisition only | Inherits Task Draft scope | Inherits final Work Order origin | Not a separate financial lane |
| Recurring Work | Prior governed relationship | Usually same-provider preference | Template plus occurrence confirmation | Inherited or explicitly updated | Reduced repeat economics where justified |
| Verified Trade | Marketplace, Provider OS, or BYOP | Often required | Provider diagnostic and estimate | Same origin fields | Referral/estimate first; payment only after external gates |

### 5.3 Provider OS — LOCKED

Provider OS is transaction-first provider retention and customer operations, not standalone generic SaaS at launch.

- V1 monthly subscription: $0.
- Core estimates, customer records, scheduling, evidence, and recurring tools are included for approved providers.
- HustleXP earns on successful governed transactions.
- Provider-originated relationship attribution is preserved.
- A paid subscription is deferred until independent recurring software value is demonstrated.

### 5.4 Task Opportunities — LOCKED

Before approval, the only provider authority is `EXPRESS_INTEREST`.

Expressing interest creates no reservation, assignment, address access, direct customer contact, financial commitment, payable, or guaranteed earning. A Task Opportunity must derive from a real Task Draft and remain redacted until eligibility and lifecycle gates permit disclosure.

### 5.5 Recurring Work — LOCKED

Every occurrence is a separate transaction with its own service date, scope version, assigned provider, Financial Security Event, evidence, capture, funding state, and reconciliation. Annual prepayment and indefinite authorization are excluded from Universal V1.

## 10. Verified Trade Partner policy

Google or other public reputation is a sourcing signal, not legal or task eligibility. Do not use “Google-verified contractor.”

- **HustleXP credential-verified** means the appropriate official registration, specialty, licenses/certifications, bond, insurance, workers' compensation, restrictions, service area, crew, processor, category, and task records have been checked and are current.

**Mode C — Full managed transaction:** disabled until all legal, processor, credential, sandbox, independent-review, and release gates pass.

## 11. Canonical Universal V1 lifecycle and money boundary

All Marketplace, Provider OS, BYOP, Task Opportunity, Recurring Work, and future approved trade transactions converge on one occurrence-level lifecycle:

```text
TASK_DRAFT
→ SCOPE_READY
→ QUOTED / ESTIMATE_REQUIRED
→ QUOTE_APPROVED
→ PAYMENT_METHOD_READY
→ PROVIDER_SOURCING
→ PAYMENT_ELIGIBLE
→ PROVIDER_SOFT_RESERVED
→ FINANCIAL_SECURITY_PENDING
→ FINANCIALLY_SECURED
→ WORK_ORDER_MATERIALIZED
→ ASSIGNED
→ IN_PROGRESS
→ COMPLETION_SUBMITTED
→ CAPTURE_PENDING
→ CAPTURED
→ SETTLING / PAYOUT_PENDING
→ FUNDED / PAID_OUT
→ RECONCILED
→ CLOSED
```

Trade referral and managed-estimate work may stop before `PAYMENT_METHOD_READY` and record a typed externally contracted outcome. It must not fabricate downstream payment or Work Order states.

### 11.2 Universal invariants — LOCKED

1. One immutable transaction root per occurrence.
2. PostgreSQL owns final lifecycle authority.
3. A durable Task Draft exists before opportunity or financial commitment.
4. Provider eligibility and conditional acceptance precede the Financial Security Event.
5. The Financial Security Event precedes Canonical Work Order materialization, hard assignment, and exact-address release.
6. Capture occurs only after completion, amount, incident, and customer-notice gates.
7. Capture, settlement, funding, payout, and reconciliation remain separate facts.
8. Closure requires processor and ledger agreement when a processor is involved.
9. `/OPS` is a governed control plane, not a database editor.
10. AI has no independent authority over money, assignment, identity, safety, private data, or policy.
11. Fake-processor certification precedes any approved processor sandbox certification.
12. Supabase or another overlay cannot create a competing task, payment, assignment, proof, settlement, or payout lifecycle.
13. The legacy captured-payment-before-task path remains disabled.

## 12. Pricing and unit economics

### 12.1 Pricing philosophy — LOCKED

1. One clear customer total before commitment.
2. No surprise checkout fee.
3. Provider sees scope, gross economics, applicable HustleXP fee, and expected net earnings before acceptance.
4. Platform pricing is only for bounded scope.
5. Provider estimates are used for material uncertainty.
6. No race-to-the-bottom open bidding.
7. Every fee maps to delivered value.
8. Pricing is versioned by lane, category, geography, and provider agreement.
9. AI may recommend but may not commit a final price.
10. Work that cannot support provider earnings and contribution economics is declined, bundled, repriced, or explicitly subsidized as an experiment.

### 12.3 Trade pricing — VALIDATION TARGET

- First 10–25 qualified trade opportunities may be free in exchange for outcome reporting and structured feedback.
- No fee for merely viewing a lead during the proof cohort.
- After proof, test a fixed attended-estimate fee, a credited estimate fee, or a disclosed capped success fee.
- A future full managed trade transaction may test 5%–8% of eligible service/labor revenue, excluding or separately treating taxes, permits, major equipment, fixtures, pass-through materials, and subcontractor pass-through costs.
- Provider OS trade transactions may test a 3%–5% workflow fee with no V1 subscription.

These are experiments, not processor approval or permanent price policy.

### 12.4 Minimum and provider floor — VALIDATION TARGET

- Core customer minimum: $89.
- Restricted-radius/add-on experimental minimum: $49.
- Standard automated ceiling: $1,000.
- Proposed platform-priced provider floor:

```text
max($50 minimum dispatch earnings, $30 × estimated service labor-hours)
+ approved materials or reimbursable expenses
```

### 12.5 Contribution margin — LOCKED DEFINITIONS

```text
CM1 = recognized HustleXP revenue
    − processor fees
    − transaction-specific verification/protection costs
    − refunds and dispute losses
    − incentives
    − transaction-specific recovery or guarantee costs
```

```text
CM2 = CM1
    − attributable customer acquisition
    − attributable provider acquisition
    − variable human operations and support labor
    − task-specific rework or rescue expense
```

Fixed engineering, executive overhead, and general G&A are excluded from CM2. Negative-margin jobs require an experiment ID, budget, hypothesis, maximum loss, and stop condition.

## 15. Metrics, proof gates, and expansion

### 15.1 Metric definitions — LOCKED

- **Qualified request:** legitimate, contactable, sufficiently scoped, geographically relevant, supported or reviewable, with real timing and willingness to proceed.
- **Payment-ready request:** current quote/estimate approved, terms accepted, payment-method reference supplied, schedule commitment retained. It is not financially secured or assigned.
- **Fill rate:** financially secured and assigned Work Orders divided by payment-ready requests.
- **Completion rate:** approved completions divided by assigned Work Orders.
- **Reconciled completion:** completion for which capture, fees, funding, refunds, disputes, and internal ledger agree with processor evidence.
- **Repeat customer:** second paid task or recurring occurrence within the defined cohort window.
- **Founder rescue:** founder action required to save a transaction that is not executable through documented SOP, delegated operations, or deterministic system behavior.

General-service and licensed-trade cohorts remain separate. A high-value trade project must not distort general-service average order value or funnel conclusions.

### 15.2 First 100 completed outcomes — VALIDATION TARGET

The cohort is the first 100 completed and reconciled paid tasks. Failed, cancelled, unfilled, and declined demand remains in the appropriate denominators.

| Metric | Success by task 100 | Reshape/stop signal |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Paid-request fill rate | ≥85% | <70% after 20 payment-ready requests |
| Assignment-to-completion | ≥90% | <85% over a 20-task cohort |
| On-time within window | ≥85% | <75% over 20 tasks |
| Provider-caused cancellation/no-show | <8% | >12% over 20 assigned tasks |
| Refund/rework incidence | <8% | >12% over 20 completions |
| Payment disputes | <2% | >5% or repeated preventable disputes |
| Reconciliation | 100% within 2 business days | Any unexplained aged mismatch |
| Repeat/recurring within 60 days | ≥20% of eligible customers | <10% after 50 matured customers |
| Provider earnings | Median ≥$30/service hour | Persistent rejection or churn |
| Positive CM1 | ≥90% of tasks | Negative for 10 comparable tasks |
| Trailing 30-task CM2 | ≥0 by task 100 | No improvement by task 75 |
| Human operations | Median ≤20 minutes/task | >30 minutes by task 50 |
| Founder rescue | <15% of tasks 76–100 | >30% after task 50 |
| Supply resilience | Five providers with 5+ completions | One provider >40% fulfillment |
| Severe incidents | Zero unresolved P0 | Repeated safety, privacy, or money-integrity failure |

Review and replace targets with evidence after outcomes 25, 50, and 100.

### 15.3 Expansion gate — LOCKED METHOD; VALIDATION TARGET THRESHOLDS

Expand one dimension at a time: existing categories into a new geography or a new category within the existing geography. Do not expand both simultaneously.

A cell should not be called scaled until it has at least ten completed/reconciled tasks, three active eligible providers, 80% fill, 90% assignment-to-completion, positive CM1, controlled incidents, declining intervention, and observable repeat/referral/recurring demand.

### 15.4 Twelve-month target — VALIDATION TARGET

Measured from the first approved production transaction:

> **By month 12, HustleXP reaches 300 safely completed and reconciled tasks per month, approximately $75,000 monthly completed GMV, at least 35% repeat or recurring work, positive trailing-90-day CM2, and less than 10% founder rescue.**

Supporting targets: fill ≥85%, assignment-to-completion ≥90%, provider-caused cancellation <8%, daily reconciliation 100%, zero unresolved P0 money/privacy/safety incidents, median operations ≤15 minutes/task, three strong category cells, and no single provider above 25% of completed GMV.

These numbers are ambition and test gates, not current performance claims.
